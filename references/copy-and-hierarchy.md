# Copy, CTA, hierarchy, and objections

Contents: value proposition and headline · CTA copy · CTA hierarchy · visual hierarchy · objection handling · message research · writing copy alternatives

These are heuristics for finding problems and drafting options. Tag findings Observed or Inferred; none of this proves lift. For full copy rewrites hand off to **copywriting**.

## Value proposition and headline

Check:
- Can a cold visitor say what this is, for whom, and why it beats the alternative within about 5 seconds?
- Is the primary benefit specific and differentiated, in the customer's words, not company jargon?
- Does the headline match the promise of the traffic source (ad, query, link)?

Common problems: feature-led instead of outcome-led; too clever or too vague; trying to say everything; a headline that needs the subhead to be understood.

Patterns worth drafting from (adapt, don't copy blindly):
- **Outcome without pain**: "Get [outcome] without [pain]".
- **Specificity**: a number, a timeframe, or a concrete detail the reader can picture.
- **Proof in the headline**: "Join [real count] [people like you] who..." only with a true, checkable number.
- **Audience call-out**: name who it is for ("for designers who...").

A headline test: replace the product name with a competitor's. If it still reads true, it is not differentiated.

Evidence note: a "5-second test" measures first impression (NN/g). The NN/g attention figure is that the first 10 seconds decide whether visitors stay and a clear value proposition keeps them longer (2011, older Microsoft Research dwell-time data). Plain language helps: NN/g (2005, n=50) recommends roughly a 6th-grade reading level for homepages and landing pages, and Unbounce's 2024 data correlates simpler copy with higher conversion (correlational; finance is an exception).

## CTA copy

State the result or next step, not the mechanism.
- Weak: "Submit", "Send", "Sign Up", "Learn More".
- Stronger: [action] + [what they get] ("Get my free quote", "See pricing", "Download the guide").
- Match the commitment to the visitor's readiness: a cold visitor will click "See how it works" before "Buy now".
- When the button leads to a form and not a payment, avoid wording that implies payment ("Pay", "Register" may mislead). Say what happens next.
- Check the label against what happens after the click (the page, the form, the call).
- Generic labels have low information scent ("More", "Learn more"). "Get Started" misled users who wanted information in an 8-user NN/g study (2017, qualitative): 6 clicked it to find out what the service was. Name the destination or the result.

## CTA hierarchy

- One clear primary action per screen, with a visible secondary for visitors not ready.
- Primary and secondary must look different. Two equal-weight CTAs for different readiness levels is fine only if their roles are clear.
- Repeat the primary CTA at decision points (after proof, after the offer, at the end), not after every paragraph.
- Count competing actions: nav items, social links, banners, popups. More actions dilute the primary one.
- Check mobile separately: is the primary CTA reachable without hunting?

## Visual hierarchy and scannability

- A scanner should get the main message from the headline, subhead, one visual, and the CTA.
- The most important element should be the most prominent; check size, contrast, and position.
- Images should support the message (product, outcome, people like the visitor), not decorate. Stock imagery that says nothing is a finding.
- Headings should tell the story in order of the visitor's questions.
- White space and grouping: crowded blocks and walls of text hurt scanning; too little content can hide the offer. Judge by whether the key message survives a 5-second look, not by a spacing rule.
- Contrast and text size: check button, body text, and light-on-image text.

## Objection handling

Name the objections a visitor would have at this price and commitment, then check whether the page answers each where it arises.

| Objection | Ways to address (only with real material) |
|---|---|
| Price or value ("is it worth it?") | Price framing against alternatives, what is included, payment plan or installments, ROI example with transparent assumptions |
| "Will this work for my situation?" | Who it is and is not for, examples from similar people, FAQ |
| Difficulty or time ("can I actually do this?") | Process transparency, support included, effort stated honestly |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | Guarantee, trial, refund or cancellation terms (only terms the seller truly offers) |
| Trust ("is this legit?") | Real contact details, company info, reviews, policies, security cues at payment |
| Timing ("why now?") | True dates or deadlines only |

Sources for the real objections: sales calls, support or WhatsApp messages, reviews, survey answers. If you don't have them, label the list Inferred and ask for the questions people actually ask.

## Message research before rewriting

For pages whose traffic is too low to A/B test, use qualitative message research: ICP-matched reviewers (panel services such as Wynter; speed claims are vendor claims), on-page surveys at the decision step, and sales-call or support-message interviews. CXL's copy-testing step recommends 15-20 interviews; one panel vendor recommends about 15 participants per page per segment. These establish clarity and objection problems; they don't measure lift. Lifts reported from message testing (+43% to +73% in vendor case studies) bundle copy and design changes and omit baseline and significance (L). Objections found in research are best answered inline at the decision step; that pattern is documented but the lifts are agency-reported.

## Writing copy alternatives

- Give 2-3 alternatives for one element (headline, CTA, or form button), each with a one-line rationale and the principle it uses.
- Draft only from the stated offer. Never invent proof points, numbers, testimonials, or guarantees.
- Label them drafts. Say whether the change is a ship or a test candidate.
- Keep the original tone and language of the page (for Turkish pages see [turkey-market](turkey-market.md)).
